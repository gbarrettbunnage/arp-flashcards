Question 1

What would you advise a listed firm to disclose as the minimum content in an interim financial report in accordance with IAS 34 Interim Financial Reporting?





a) Full financial statements with detailed notes and management analysis.





b) Only a condensed statement of financial position and a detailed management commentary.





c) Condensed financial statements, including statements of financial position, profit or loss and other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows, along with basic and diluted earnings per share and comparatives.





d) A detailed cashflow forecast for the remainder of the financial year and preliminary results for the next fiscal period.



The correct answer is c.  



IAS 34 requires a set of condensed financial statements that include essential elements such as the statement of financial position, profit or loss, changes in equity, and cash flows, alongside earnings per share and comparatives, providing a snapshot of the company's performance and position during the interim period.



Question 2 and 3



Use the following information to answer the next two questions.



&#x20;



Suzanne Ltd 



Suzanne Ltd (Suzanne) has historically operated three main divisions: Consumer Electronics, Industrial Equipment, and Mobile Devices. Over the past few years, the Mobile Devices division has struggled to remain competitive due to intense market competition and rapid technological changes. On 15th July 20X5, Suzanne’s board of directors decided to sell the Mobile Devices division, deeming it non-core to the company's strategic focus. They announced the decision publicly on 20 July 20X5 and actively began seeking buyers on that date. They entered into a contractual sale with Lynn Ltd (Lynn) on 30 November 20X5 for £40m. The sale was successfully completed on 31 December 20X5. At the time of sale, the Mobile Devices division had net assets with a carrying amount of £35m.







For the financial year ending 31 December 20X5, Suzanne reported the following in its draft financial statements: 







Total revenue: £150m



Total net assets: £120m







Included in these totals, the Mobile Devices division contributed £20m to revenue and had net assets with a carrying amount of £35m. The division's operations resulted in a net loss of £5m for the year.



How should Suzanne report the sale of its Mobile Devices division in the financial statements for the year ending 31 December 20X5 according to IFRS 5 Non-current assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations?





a) Integrate the loss from the Mobile Devices division's operations within the profit or loss from continuing operations and disclose the sale in the notes.





b) Report the net loss from the Mobile Devices division's operations and the gain on sale as part of the profit or loss from continuing operations.





c) Present the net loss from the Mobile Devices division's operations separately in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income under discontinued operations, and disclose the details of the sale in the notes.





d) Exclude the Mobile Devices division's financial results from the year's financial statements and only disclose the sale in the next fiscal year's reports.



The correct answer is c.



IFRS 5 requires that the results of discontinued operations be presented separately in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income. This includes any profit or loss from the operations of the component (the division in this case) up until the disposal date and any gain or loss on the sale of the component. Additional disclosures regarding the discontinued operation should be made in the notes to the financial statements, providing transparency about the impact of the divestiture on the company's financial results.



Based on the provided figures, what is the total amount reported in Suzanne’s statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 20X5 in respect of the profit or loss for the year from the discontinued operations, according to IFRS 5 Non-current assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations?





a) £0m





b) £5m loss





c) £5m gain 





d) £10m gain



The correct answer is a.  



According to IFRS 5, the total impact on profit from discontinued operations includes the operating results up to the disposal date and the gain or loss on sale. The division operated at a loss of £5m for the year. However, the sale resulted in a gain of £40m (sale price) – £35m (net assets) = £5m. Therefore, the net impact on profit from the discontinued operations is a £0m (£5m gain on sale minus £5m operational loss). That it nets to £0 does not mean that the information is not disclosed.

Question 9 and 10



Use the following information to answer the next two questions.







Carman Ltd



You are the financial controller of Carman Ltd (Carman), a private company that produces a range of cakes. The finance director has asked for your advice regarding the disclosure requirements of IAS 24 Related Party Disclosures.



Orange plc (Orange) owns 80% of the ordinary share capital of Carman. Carman owns 15% of the ordinary share capital of Stanmore Ltd (Stanmore) and 30% of the ordinary share capital of Lyle Ltd (Lyle).



Carman has four directors: Black, Brown, Blue and Green. Three of the directors are involved in other businesses. Black and Brown are the two partners of Beige LLP, a management consultancy partnership. Green’s daughter owns 70% of the ordinary share capital of Chocs-away Ltd (Chocs-away), a retailer of confectionery and cakes.



The following transactions took place during the year ended 30 June 20X4. Carman sold goods to Orange for £400,000 and bought £100,000 and £250,000 of goods from Stanmore and Lyle respectively. Beige supplied management consultancy services to Carman at a discounted rate of £80,000. A company car with a carrying amount of £15,000 was sold to the spouse of Blue for £12,000. Carman had surplus office space which it allowed Lyle to use, free of charge. The stand-alone selling price is £40,000. Carman sold goods to Chocs-away for £95,000 at a markup of 25%. Carman’s stand-alone selling price for these goods is based on a markup of 30%.



Which of the following are considered to be related parties of Carman as per IAS 24? Select ALL that apply.





a) Orange





b) Lyle





c) Stanmore





d) The four directors – Black, Brown, Blue and Green





e) The daughter of Green





f) The spouse of Blue





g) Chocs-away





h) Beige LLP



All the alternatives are correct except c.



The following are related parties:



Orange and Lyle, as members of the same group 

The four directors, as members of key management personnel of Carman 

The daughter of Green and spouse of Blue, as they are close members of the family of a related party 

Chocs-away Ltd, as an entity controlled by a close member of the family of a related party 

Beige LLP and Carman are not related simply because they share two members of key management personnel.



However, as Beige is jointly controlled by a member of key management personnel of Carman, they are related parties.



Stanmore is not a related party, as the investment held by Carman is not significant.



Which transactions should Carman disclose as part of IAS 24 Related Party Disclosures? Select ALL that apply.





a) Trading of goods with Orange, but no amounts





b) Trading of goods with Orange, including amounts





c) Trading of goods with Lyle, but no amounts 





d) Trading of goods with Lyle, including amounts





e) Trading of goods with Stanmore, but no amounts





f) Trading of goods with Stanmore, including amounts





g) Trading of goods with Chocs-away, but no amounts





h) Trading of goods with Chocs-away, including amounts and reduced markup





i) Management consultancy services received, including amounts and recognition of it being discounted





j) Sale of company car, including amounts and recognition of a sale beneath carrying amount





k) The fair value of office space that Lyle used free of charge



The correct answers are: b, d, h, i, j, k. 







The nature of the related party relationship, as described, must be disclosed along with:



The amount of the transactions 



The amount of outstanding balances, including commitments 



 Specifically, the following should be disclosed:



Trading of goods with Orange, Lyle and Chocs-away, including amounts and recognition of reduced markup (for Chocs-away) 



Management consultancy services received, including amounts and recognition of it being discounted 



Sale of company car, including amounts and recognition of a sale beneath carrying amount 



The fair value of office space that Lyle used free of charge
