The correct answer is b.

Palau plc (Palau) is a multinational pharmaceuticals company with operations in the UK and the European Union.  



&#x20;



Palau is an early adopter of ESRS and prepares a comprehensive sustainability report in accordance with them. The finance director of Palau has been recently appointed and does not have experience in ESRS, although they did attend a comprehensive training course covering the requirements of the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards in their previous role. 



&#x20;



Palau has an excellent reputation for the treatment of its employees and is regularly named as a top company to work for. Palau recently decided to outsource its labelling and packaging operations to a third-party supplier. The supplier pays the national minimum wage to its employees and has been subject to negative media attention due to ongoing strikes related to employees having relatively poor working conditions and being expected to work overtime at short notice without enhanced rates of pay.  



&#x20;



Palau’s financial controller has asked the finance director to draft sustainability-related disclosures in relation to the third-party supplier. The finance director does not understand why such disclosures are needed as the training course they attended did not mention such disclosures. 



&#x20;



Required: 



a) Briefly explain the approach to materiality taken by the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and ESRS.







b) Evaluate whether disclosures relating to the third-party supplier would be required in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards.  







c) Explain, making brief reference to the approach to materiality, why disclosures relating to the third-party supplier may be required under ESRS.



Solution

a) IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards apply financial materiality when determining whether sustainability-related risks and opportunities require disclosure. Financial materiality is defined in the same way as in the Conceptual Framework, in that information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could impact on decisions made by the primary users. IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards require entities to provide sustainability-related financial disclosures, ie information about risks and opportunities which affect the entity’s financial prospects. European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) apply double materiality and therefore require entities to provide information about issues which affect the entity’s financial prospects or about the impact of the entity’s operations on the environment and society, or issues which affect both financial prospects and the impact of the entity’s operations.  







b) The issue with the third-party supplier does not have a direct financial impact on Palau as there is no suggestion that the service the third party provides to Palau will be impacted by the employee relations issues it faces. There are no sustainability-related financial risks to Palau arising from this issue and therefore no specific disclosures would be required under the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards.  







c) The issue with the third-party supplier is disclosed under ESRS as the impact of an entity’s operations is not limited to the entity itself but also includes its value chain, and therefore third-party suppliers. If the issues with this supplier are considered material to any group of stakeholders identified by Palau, the issue may need to be disclosed.

What is the definition of ‘fair presentation’ in accordance with IFRS S1 and how does it compare to the definition of fair presentation provided in the Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting?







Solution

In order to meet the definition of fair presentation in accordance with IFRS S1, disclosures must include all material sustainability-related risks and opportunities that could be reasonably expected to affect the entity’s prospects. The information must be relevant and faithfully represent the sustainability-related risks and opportunities. Information should be complete, neutral and accurate.







The Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting includes faithful representation as one of the fundamental qualitative characteristics rather than fair presentation. Information must present the substance of the relevant phenomena it purports to represent. Consistent with IFRS S1, the characteristics of complete, neutral and free from error are required to achieve faithful representation.

IFRS S1 states that information should be relevant and should faithfully represent reality – being complete, neutral and accurate.







Suggest two ways in which the information in Southwark’s disclosures might not be complete, neutral or accurate. 



Solution

Southwark’s disclosures have the following shortcomings, with reference to the qualitative characteristics of information identified in IFRS S1:



Southwark is using deliberately vague language in order to create an overall impression of sustainability, which might not be realistic.  



Southwark has also used stereotypical and suggestive images meant to convey a message of natural harmony (trees, feathers), which might bear no relation to its activities of selling industrially produced food. 



Southwark states that it is increasing the proportion of packaging which is recycled or can be recycled and which comes from sustainable sources. However, it does not disclose any quantitative data so the user has no idea what proportion of packaging is recycled, can be recycled or comes from sustainable sources, and whether the rate of each is increasing.   



Southwark states that it is committed to reducing the overall weight of its packaging without stating whether it has reduced it and, if so, by how much, and it does not include any specific targets or actual quantitative data which would allow the user to assess the company’s performance against its targets. 



Southwark uses a meaningless phrase, “Planet Friendly Packaging”, meant to convey positive associations with the environment. 



Southwark states that a percentage of its brands use Planet Friendly Packaging but does not state any absolute amounts or proportions in terms of volume of trade.



Suggest two improvements to Southwark’s disclosures.



Possible improvements could be:



Include quantitative data on the amount of packaging used and the proportion of packaging which is recycled, recyclable or made from sustainable sources. 



Include comparative data from previous years so users can view progress. 



Include information about targets and actual figures so users can assess progress towards targets. 



Include objective information on what is meant by phrases such as “Planet Friendly Packaging” and “sustainable sources”.



Neesia plc (Neesia) includes sustainability-related financial information in its annual report in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. The directors of Neesia are unclear on whether this information is required to be subject to assurance. The managing director has expressed the view that if external assurance is required, he expects that the company’s auditors will consider this information as part of their audit of the financial statements as the information is financial in nature.







Briefly advise whether assurance of sustainability-related financial information prepared in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards is required and, if so, whether it would be part of the financial statement audit process.



Solution

There is no requirement for information prepared in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards to be subject to external assurance, unless required by local regulators. As the information is presented in the annual report, it will be read by the company’s auditors to ensure it is consistent with their understanding, but it will not be subject to audit testing. Neesia may decide to request assurance of its sustainability information to enhance its credibility, although there would need to be a cost-benefit analysis to determine if this is worthwhile. If it did decide to request assurance, this would be separate from the statutory audit of the financial statements.

Tuvala plc (Tuvala) surveyed its employees in its annual employee satisfaction survey. It has received feedback indicating that the company does not take sustainability matters seriously enough. In response, the company has decided to prepare information about aspects of sustainability that are likely to be of most interest to employees. It is considering whether to apply the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards or GRI Standards when preparing this information.



Evaluate whether sustainability-related information prepared in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards or GRI Standards is more likely to satisfy Tuvala’s employees.



Solution

IFRS Sustainability Disclosures Standards are intended to provide sustainability-related financial information relating to Tuvala’s sustainability-related risks and opportunities. Such information is useful to the primary users of general purpose financial reports in understanding how sustainability-related risks and opportunities might impact on the entity’s prospects. This is unlikely to satisfy Tuvala’s employees who are not part of the primary user group.



Employees are a stakeholder group whose information needs would be considered under GRI Standards. If Tuvala was to apply GRI Standards, it would consider the issues that are most important to stakeholders, including employees, and would include information on these issues in its sustainability report. Information prepared in accordance with GRI Standards is therefore more likely to satisfy Tuvala’s employees.



Which engagement channel provides the least in-depth opportunity for gathering stakeholder feedback as part of a materiality process?





a) Meetings or workshops with management/functional representatives and meetings of a corporate sustainability committee





b) Facilitated focus groups or workshops





c) Meetings with targeted stakeholders





d) One-on-one interviews





e) Online surveys



The correct answer is e. 







Online surveys provide a great way to engage a large number of stakeholders, internal and external to the entity, and to gather feedback in a consistent way across similar questions, which enhances comparability. However, they are limited by the nature of the questions that can be asked to avoid survey fatigue and, as they are not interactive, follow-up questions cannot be asked. The results also reflect varying levels of knowledge and understanding of the entity, its operations and impacts.  



Boxhill Ltd (Boxhill) is an engineering company suppling parts to vehicle manufacturers. The company prepares sustainability disclosures in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. For the first time this year, it is also preparing a separate sustainability report in accordance with GRI Standards.

Suggest two other stakeholder groups that Boxhill could identify for reporting with reference to GRI Standards.   



Suggest one other method of engagement that Boxhill could use to engage with a range of stakeholder groups which is likely to be more cost effective than one-to-one interviews and focus groups. 



Solution

Employees and/or their trade union representatives and local community groups (eg residents affected by the operation of the manufacturing plants) are likely to be the most relevant stakeholders for Boxhill.



Surveys and/or questionnaires, either online or sent by post, are likely to be the cheapest way of engaging with a range of stakeholder groups. Other methods, such as targeted meetings, interviews, focus groups, and workshops, may give more detailed and specific information but will be more expensive and more resource intensive.

##### Lesson 4: Climate-Related Disclosures



Which of the following have specific requirements for identifying climate-related risks and opportunities?



Select all that apply.





a) IFRS S2





b) GRI Standards





c) ESRS



The correct answer is a and c.



IFRS S2 (a) and ESRS (c) have requirements for identifying climate-related risks and opportunities (CROs). The GRI Standards (b) do not have prescriptive requirements for identifying CROs. However, in determining potential material topics, CROs are likely to be identified with their impacts assessed as part of the materiality process.



Which of the following statements is true?  





a) IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and ESRS are consistent, but ESRS require more disclosures





b) IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and ESRS are consistent, but ISSB Standards require more disclosures



The correct answer is a.







IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and ESRS are highly aligned around disclosures that provide information for the primary users of financial statements about the impact of risks and opportunities on an entity’s financial performance and prospects. But IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards are based on financial materiality, whereas ESRS are based on double materiality. Therefore, ESRS contain additional requirements for information relevant to stakeholders interested in the impact of an entity’s activities.



Which of following risks is/are likely to be classified as a transition risk by an agricultural producer?





a) Temperature changes affect crop viability





b) Flood risk increasing because of weather patterns





c) Government policy changes restricting use of energy





d) Spread of pests





The correct answer is c.







Policy changes are an example of a transition risk, ie a risk that arises from transition to a lower-carbon economy. All of the other options are physical risks.
