##### Lesson 1: Introduction to Groups



In the consolidated financial statements, which of the following indicates an investment in financial assets?





a) Acquisition of 100% of X Ltd





b) Acquisition of 60% of X Ltd





c) Acquisition of 7% of X Ltd





d) Acquisition of 25% of X Ltd



The correct answer is c.



Choices a and b are investments in subsidiaries while d is an investment in an associate. The correct answer is c, as the holding percentage is less than 20%.



Which of the following indicates an investment in an associate?  





a) Acquiring 30% equity holding in another company without significant influence





b) Acquiring 10% equity holding in another company and taking part in operating decisions





c) Acquiring 51% equity holding in another company





d) Acquiring 2% equity holding in another company



The correct answer is b.



The investor can exercise significant influence even with a low percentage holding. Choice a is wrong as the investor does not have significant influence. Choice c most probably indicates an investment in a subsidiary and choice d an investment in a financial asset as there is no indication of significant influence.



There are four cases on the relationship between John Ltd (John) and George Ltd (George):



John owns 45% of voting rights of George and has an agreement with another investor that holds 20% of voting rights that allows John to vote for these shares.



John owns 41% of voting rights of George but also has an agreement to control the financial and operating policies of George. 



John owns 30% of voting rights of George and has the power to appoint six out of 10 members of the board of directors. 



John owns 40% of voting rights of George and the remaining shares are held by various investors.



In which cases does John have control over George?





a) 1 and 2





b) 1, 2 and 3





c) 1, 2 and 4





d) All cases



The correct answer is b.







For cases 1, 2 and 3, John clearly controls the decisions of George, making George a subsidiary of John.







Case 4 does not provide sufficient evidence of control under the specific criteria set by IFRS 10.



GB Ltd (GB) owns most of the voting shares in a foreign operation based in Ruritania; the law in Ruritania forbids foreign investors from exercising their rights and, therefore, GB delegates its power to a third party, OB, which is based in Ruritania, and instructs OB to vote in line with its instructions.



Which party controls the foreign operation?





a) GB





b) OB



The correct answer is a.



GB has the majority of shares in the foreign operation and exposure to variable returns by virtue of being a shareholder. Despite having a majority shareholding and, therefore, power, it is unable to exercise its power. GB has power and rights to variable returns but does not have the ability to use its power to affect the returns.



Power is delegated to OB by GB. OB exercises that power in line with instructions from GB. Therefore, OB is an agent because it uses the power delegated to it to affect returns for the benefit of GB.



In this case, the decision-making rights of the agent, OB, should be treated as being held by GB (the principal directly). This means that GB does have the ability to use its power to affect returns.



Therefore, GB controls the foreign operation and should consolidate it.
