##### Lesson 1: The Strategic Report



Goodwin Plc (Goodwin) is a UK-based tyre construction company listed on the London Stock Exchange (LSE). It has 800 employees, a turnover of £40.8 million, and a statement of financial position total of £16.2 million.



Which of the following best describes how Goodwin should approach the preparation of its Strategic Report in accordance with the Companies Act 2006 and the FRC’s guidance?





a) Goodwin can exclude non-financial KPI information from its Strategic Report because, as a medium-sized company, it is exempt from such disclosure requirements.





b) Goodwin’s Strategic Report can focus on financial performance and does not need to include any information on environmental matters.





c) Goodwin’s Strategic Report should provide a comprehensive overview, including details on its strategy, business model, future development factors, environmental initiatives, and employee information. 





d) Goodwin should prepare its Strategic Report focusing only on the achievements and performance metrics for the current accounting period.



The correct answer is c.



As a quoted company, Goodwin is required to provide financial and non-financial information that reflects the directors' view of the company, including additional details on environmental matters, employee demographics, and social and community issues, thereby offering a holistic view of its performance and strategic direction. This approach facilitates transparency and informs stakeholders as to the company's comprehensive performance beyond financial metrics.



Option a) is incorrect. Goodwin is a listed company and is therefore required to include non-financial KPI information.



Option b) is incorrect as the Companies Act 2006 and the FRC’s guidance encourage a broad view of reporting that encompasses both financial and non-financial information. Option d) is incorrect because the Strategic Report is designed to provide not just a retrospective view of the company's performance but also a forward-looking perspective, including an analysis of main trends and factors likely to affect future development, performance, or position of the company’s business.



Horizon Plc (Horizon) is a publicly quoted UK company which has over 600 employees and a turnover exceeding £600 million. Horizon prepares its annual report for the financial year ended 31 December 20X3 in compliance with UK regulatory requirements.



Which of the following best describes the approach that Horizon should take to prepare its Strategic Report, in accordance with UK regulations and TCFD recommendations?





a) Horizon should mention climate-related risks in its Strategic Report but does not need to provide any further detail.





b) Horizon should place all climate-related financial disclosures in a separate document outside of the annual report, linking to it via the company website for interested stakeholders.





c) Horizon should mention climate-related risks in its Strategic Report but exclude any reference to climate-related scenarios or the potential impacts on its business model, because such information would be too speculative for inclusion in the Strategic Report. 





d) Horizon should provide a comprehensive overview of climate-related financial disclosures within the Strategic Report.



The correct answer is d.







Horizon should provide a comprehensive overview of climate-related financial disclosures within the Non-Financial and Sustainability Information Statement section of the annual report, following TCFD recommendations and UK regulations. It should cover governance, risk management, impact on business strategy, and performance against climate-related targets.



Option a) misrepresents the depth and breadth of disclosures required by UK regulations and TCFD recommendations, which necessitate a detailed discussion of climate-related risks and opportunities, not just a brief mention.



In the case of option b), while companies can disclose additional information beyond the basic requirements outside the annual report, the essential climate-related financial disclosures must be included within the Non-Financial and Sustainability Information Statement section of the annual report itself, not in separate documents.



For option c), excluding information on climate-related scenarios and their potential impacts contradicts the TCFD’s recommendations and UK regulations, which explicitly require such analysis to inform stakeholders about the resilience of the company’s business model in the face of climate change.

##### Lesson 2: Integrated Reporting





What is the primary purpose of an Integrated Report as defined by the IIRC?





a) To provide a detailed financial statement including profit or loss and other comprehensive income over a period.





b) To communicate concisely how an organisation’s strategy, governance, performance, and prospects, in the context of its external environment, lead to the creation of value in the short, medium, and long term. 





c) To provide a valuation of the organisation at a specific point in time, including its market capitalisation. 





d) To list all the organisation’s assets, including intangible assets, in their financial statements to reflect their market capitalisation.



The correct answer is b.







The primary purpose of an integrated report is to explain how an organisation creates value over time, focusing on its strategy, governance, performance, and future prospects in relation to its external environment. This approach aims to move beyond the limitations of traditional financial reporting by incorporating broader factors such as sustainability and intangible assets, which are crucial for understanding an organisation’s long-term viability.



Option a) is incorrect because, while financial statements are a component of corporate reporting, the purpose of an integrated report goes beyond just detailing financial performance. Integrated reporting seeks to present a broader picture of how value is created over time, incorporating both financial and non-financial factors. This option is too narrow and misses the essence of integrated reporting which is about the integration of various dimensions of organisational performance and strategy.



Option c) is incorrect as it misunderstands the objective of integrated reporting. Integrated reporting does not aim to monetise or provide a numerical valuation of the organisation at a point in time. Instead, it focuses on explaining how value is generated through the organisation’s business model, strategies, and use of various capitals over the short, medium, and long term.



Option d) is incorrect as listing assets, including intangible ones, to reflect market capitalisation suggests a focus that is too narrow and aligned with traditional financial reporting.

Human capital refers to an entity's management and its employees considering their competencies, capabilities, experience and motivations.

Pure Ltd (Pure) is a leader in the clean energy sector and has recently undertaken a strategic shift to not only expand its renewable energy portfolio but also to invest heavily in community development projects and advanced research into sustainable resource use. This strategic shift is underpinned by a forward-looking approach to creating value for both the organisation and its broader stakeholder community over the short, medium, and longer term. The company has initiated partnerships with local governments and non-profit organisations to ensure the effective implementation of its community projects and has committed a significant portion of its R\&D budget to explore innovative energy storage solutions.



Given Pure’s strategic shift, which aspect of its approach best illustrates the company’s alignment with the principles of integrated reporting as described in the IRF?





a) Pure focuses exclusively on increasing its financial capital through the expansion of its renewable energy portfolio, aiming to maximise short-term financial returns to its shareholders.





b) By investing in community development projects, albeit compromising the company’s financial capital, Pure effectively illustrates integrated thinking.





c) Pure’s comprehensive strategy exemplifies integrated thinking by enhancing natural, intellectual, social and relationship, and human capitals, alongside sustaining financial capital for long-term value creation.

Fury Ltd (Fury) receives government grants which are used to further the research and development of medical technologies. Fury’s directors are concerned that, due to the requirements of the IFRS Accounting Standards, the information in the financial statements and associated disclosure notes does not tell the whole story of the company’s activities. They are considering disclosing information in accordance with the Integrated Reporting Framework.



Advise, briefly, whether integrated reporting can report more useful information relating to business activities.



Solution

There are very strict criteria in IAS 38 regarding the recognition of intangible assets which can mean relevant information regarding the value of research and development is not reflected by applying the IFRS Accounting Standards. An integrated report can be a useful way to communicate the value of intangible assets. Disclosing information in an integrated report would allow Fury to disclose the change in value of an unrecognised intangible asset as a result of its business activities. For example, it can report its increase in intellectual capitals (knowledge and know-how) as a result of utilising its financial capital (grant received).



You are the financial controller of Vine Ltd (Vine). The directors have asked for your advice regarding materiality. They understand financial materiality in the context of the financial statements, but have heard the term ‘double materiality’ now being used in reference to some sustainability reporting. When considering whether to prepare an integrated report, they have also heard that materiality has a different definition under the IntegratedReporting Framework.



Advise, briefly, on the definition of materiality in the Integrated Reporting Framework and how it compares to financial materiality and double materiality.



Solution

In the IRF, a matter is considered material if it could substantively affect the organisation’s ability to create value over time. Financial materiality is focused on the relevance of an omission or misstatement in the organisation’s financial statements to the primary users of the financial statements. Double materiality, as is used when reporting under European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), applies both financial materiality and impact materiality, which considers the impact of an entity on the environment and society.



Matters which are considered material under financial or double materiality may also be material for integrated reporting purposes if they are of such relevance and importance that they could change the assessments of providers of financial capital with regard to the organisation’s ability to create value.

Which two of the following represent an approach Sulphur could take to ensure its reporting is in line with stakeholder expectations?





a) Sulphur could prepare an Integrated Report which will help it to illustrate how sustainability-related matters impact overall value creation.





b) Sulphur could include some key non-financial indicators in the narrative parts of its annual report.





c) Sulphur could prepare separate financial and non-financial reports, as they cater to different stakeholder interests.





d) Sulphur could enhance its Strategic Report by placing significant emphasis on detailing its sustainability-related matters and connecting sustainability with its financial reporting. 



The correct answer is a and d.



Explanation



Option a) is correct since it aligns with the aim of providing a holistic view of the company’s performance. Integrating financial and non-financial information ensures that the report comprehensively covers all aspects of the company’s operations and strategy, including the impact of sustainability-related matters on financial stability and value creation.



Option d) is correct since it suggests a balanced approach that not only emphasises the importance of sustainability-related information but also connects that information directly with financial performance, providing a holistic perspective.



Option b) is incorrect since it still places primary emphasis on financial reporting, treating non-financial indicators as secondary or supplementary. Although it suggests the inclusion of non-financial data, the approach does not fully commit to integrating these elements.



Option c) is incorrect since it suggests keeping financial and non-financial information distinct and separate, which can lead to a fragmented view of the company’s operations. It also does not meet the needs of stakeholders who seek a holistic understanding of financial and non-financial matters.



Energa Ltd (Energa) is an oil and gas company which undertook an evaluation of PPE in light of evolving climate policies and market preferences shifting towards renewable energy sources. The company acknowledges the material risk of its assets becoming stranded in the future.



How should Energa address the risk of stranded assets in its financial reporting?





a) Energa should ignore the potential impacts of climate policies and market preferences on its assets as the assets are not yet stranded and the information impacts the assets in the future.





b) Energa should carry out an impairment review on its assets as required under IAS 36, disclosing in the notes any judgements made around the impact of climate policies and market shifts on asset valuation, and its strategies for risk mitigation.





c) Energa should write off the entire carrying amount of its assets immediately to pre-empt future impairments and shifts in the energy market.





d) Energa should not change the carrying amount of the assets but should create a provision for onerous assets which will be presented in its statement of financial position.



The correct answer is b.



Option b) is correct because it accurately reflects how Energa has addressed the risk of stranded assets in accordance with IAS 36 Impairment of Assets.



Option a) is incorrect as ignoring the impacts of climate policies and market shifts contradicts the principles of prudent financial reporting and risk assessment required by IAS 36.



Option c) is incorrect since writing off the entire carrying amount of assets immediately would be an overly aggressive response that does not align with the requirements of IAS 36.



Option d) is incorrect as creating a provision would not be in keeping with the requirements of IAS 37.

Flintstone Ltd (Flintstone) is a company operating in the construction sector. Recent environmental impact assessments and market trends indicate that the risk of regulatory changes and physical risks (including erratic weather patterns and natural disasters) indicated an impairment of Flintstone’s PPE. The company prepares financial statements in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards and sustainability disclosures in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards.



Advise on the disclosures required under IFRS Accounting Standards and IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and how reporting consistency can be achieved.



Solution

Disclosures under IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards







The impairment would be accounted for in the financial statements resulting in a loss in the statement of profit or loss and a decrease in the carrying amount of the assets. The following disclosures are required in accordance with IAS 36:







IAS 36 requires disclosure of the events and circumstances leading to the impairment. This will include a: 



Description of the specific risk factors that have been identified (eg potential regulatory changes, erratic weather patterns, natural disasters). 

Explanation of how these risks could potentially affect the carrying amount of PPE. 

Disclosure of significant judgements made in assessing the risk of impairment.

Disclosures under IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards



Under IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards, Flintstone needs to discuss the sustainability-related risks that may impact the prospects of Flintstone, as well as the mitigation strategies. This affects various aspects of reporting under IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. Specifically:



Governance



Description of the governance processes in place for managing environmental and regulatory risks. 

Strategy 



Description of the potential impacts of regulatory changes and physical risks on the company’s business model, strategy, and financial planning over the short, medium, and long term. 

Risk Management 



Explanation of how the company identifies, assesses, and manages sustainability-related risks. 

Description of the processes in place to monitor these risks and integrate them into overall risk management. 

Achieving consistency between financial and sustainability-related reporting



Needs to be ensured that the descriptions of risks that lead to the impairment (under IAS 36) are aligned with the risks described in the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. The same terminology and risk assessments should be used across both reports.



Cross-referencing between financial statements and sustainability-related reports is needed. For example, financial statements should mention where further details on the identified risks can be found in the sustainability report, and vice versa.

What responsibilities does a CA have if they suspect a report they are helping to prepare includes greenwashing? 



Choose the option(s) you think are correct, then select ‘Submit’ for feedback.





a) CAs are obliged to behave with integrity so if greenwashing is observed the CA must take action





b) CAs are required to assess and verify if information is faithfully represented





c) CAs are experienced in interpreting disclosure standards and should ensure claims against standards are met





d) CAs are unbiased and should ensure that balanced information is presented regarding sustainability performance



All of the answer options are correct. The ICAS Code of Ethics requires CAs to be objective, act diligently with professional competence and due care and maintain professional behaviour. CAs are empowered with the responsibility to ensure claims are substantiated and that disclosures are faithfully represented and that an entity fairly presents its approach to and performance in addressing its impacts and sustainability-related risks and opportunities.



How can an entity disclosing a claim of sustainability performance, or environmental or social responsibility, substantiate its claim? 



A reporting entity could clearly and comprehensively disclose why and what it is claiming. If it is a claim related to sustainability performance, outline what the attributes are of the project or service that is being claimed and substantiate this from a sustainability impact point of view. For example, this could involve explaining how impacts are avoided or reduced over an alternative, what social benefit arises to suppliers, workers, SMEs or communities, and how claims are substantiated through a certification scheme, governance oversight, due diligence or assurance.



How can an entity disclosing performance against a target substantiate the credibility of its disclosure?







Solution

A reporting entity could accurately state what the target covers in terms of the scope of its operations, including along the value chain if applicable, and the nature of the operations or activities included in the scope of the target. If the target does not cover all aspects of the entity’s operations or activities, this should be clearly explained along with a rationale for what is included and what isn’t. For example, the entity could include all buildings in its portfolio that it has operational/managerial control over and explain that. Material changes in its operations or activities that affect the achievement of the target, positively or negatively, should be disclosed.
