##### Lesson 1: Lease contracts



On 1 August 20X4 Lisbon Ltd (Lisbon) entered into a contract to obtain the use of a lorry. The registration plate of the lorry is identified in the agreement and Lisbon can control the use of the lorry. The agreement permits Lisbon to use the lorry to drive a total of 10,000 miles and the company will make monthly bank payments for its use. The payments will be linked to the monthly mileage at a fixed price per mile.



Which statement is correct?





a) The period of use is identified in the contract, however, the contract is not a lease because consideration is not at a fixed price per month.





b) The contract identifies consideration payable, however, the contract is not a lease because the period of use is not identified in the contract.





c) The contract identifies neither the period of use nor consideration and therefore it is not a lease.





d) The contract identifies both the period of use and the consideration and therefore it is a lease.



The correct answer is d.



Payments of consideration need not be at fixed amounts 

The period of use can be specified in terms of output (here mileage) rather than time



During May 20X4 Sofia Ltd (Sofia), which publishes advertising flyers, obtained the use of a ‘green’ printer for three years in exchange for monthly payments. The printer is designed to produce fewer emissions and waste than traditional printers and is adapted to use eco-inks. The terms of the contract prohibit Sofia from using traditional inks as these may damage the printer. They also require that a maximum of 48,000 pages be printed each year. Monthly payments include a fixed amount plus a ‘per page’ charge for every page printed over 2,500.



Which statement is incorrect?





a) Sofia can obtain substantially all economic benefits from the printer despite the restriction on the number of pages that can be printed.





b) The requirement to pay a ‘per page’ charge to the supplier means that Sofia cannot access substantially all economic benefits from the use of the printer.





c) Despite the restriction on the number of pages that can be printed, Sofia can direct the use of the printer.





d) The requirement to use eco-inks rather than traditional inks does not affect Sofia’s ability to direct the use of the printer.



The correct answer is b.



The restriction on the number of pages that can be printed is a parameter that is set out in the contract. Within this parameter, Sofia can obtain substantially all economic benefits from the printer and direct its use.







Sofia accesses substantially all economic benefits from the use of the printer; it then has to transfer some of these to the supplier in the way of payment, however, this is not relevant to an assessment of access to economic benefits.







The requirement to use eco-inks rather than traditional inks is a protective right, designed to protect the printer. This does not mean that Sofia does not have the right to direct the use of the printer.

Is the contract a lease?

Period of use 



The contract is for a period of time of 20 years. 



Consideration 



Consideration is required to be paid quarterly. 



Identified asset 



The asset obtained by Rome is not physically distinct as it is only 30% of the capacity rather than substantially all of it. 



Right to control the use of the asset – direct the use 



Rome has the right to use 30% of the cable, however it is unlikely to be able to control this. Any decisions about how or when the cable is used will be made at a whole-asset level and Rome is unlikely to have any involvement in these decisions.

Rome has the right to obtain the economic benefits associated with the 30% of the cable that it has obtained use of. &#x20;

In 20X6 Athens Ltd (Athens) entered a contract to obtain the use of a unit within a large storage facility for four years in exchange for fixed consideration. Athens Ltd’s right to direct the use of the unit is unaffected by: 



the clause designed to protect the owner by prohibiting the storage of dangerous or illegal goods. Within this restriction it can control how the unit is used and what is stored there. 

the opening hours of the unit. Within the parameters of these hours, it can access the unit whenever it wishes.

##### Lesson 5: Sale and leaseback



Belgrade Ltd (Belgrade) has a property with a carrying amount of £12.5m on 1 January 20X1. The remaining useful life of the property is 34 years. On 1 January 20X1, Belgrade sold the property to a bank at its fair value of £18m. It immediately leased back the property for 8 years, agreeing to pay an annual lease payment of £900,000, on 31 December each year. The transfer is a sale and the rate of interest implicit in the lease is 7%. The 8-year 7% annuity factor is 5.971.



What gain should Belgrade recognise on the sale of the property?





a) £1.65million





b) £3.135million





c) £3.85million





d) £5.5million



The correct answer is c.







The lease liability at 1 January 20X1 is calculated as £5,373,900 (£900,000 x 5.971).







The proportion of the asset that is retained is 30% (£5,373,900/£18,000,000 x 100%).







Therefore the proportion of the asset that is transferred is 70%. 







The full gain on disposal is £5.5m (£18m - £12.5m), however only 70% of this is recognised on the transfer, ie £3.85m.



Bilbao Ltd (Bilbao) sold a specialised item of production equipment to EuroBank on 1 July 20X6 for its fair value of £750,000 when the equipment had a carrying amount of £720,000. It immediately leased the equipment back in order to continue using it, in exchange for 5 annual payments of £165,000, starting on 30 June 20X7. The production equipment’s remaining useful life on 1 July 20X6 was 6 years after which it was expected to become obsolete due to technological advances. Bilbao is required to provide insurance and maintenance for the equipment throughout the lease term.







The financial accountant of Bilbao has recently returned to work after an extended leave of absence and is unsure how to account for this transaction.







How would you explain the required accounting treatment?



Solution

The transaction involves selling an asset for proceeds equal to its fair value and then leasing the asset back.



IFRS 16 Leases applies and requires that the first step is to consider whether the transfer of the equipment to Eurobank is a sale in accordance with IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers.



IFRS 15 states that a sale occurs when control of an asset transfers to the customer (Eurobank in this case). It goes on to provide indicators of a sale.



One of the key indicators is whether the risks and rewards of ownership have transferred from seller to customer and in particular which of those parties has access to substantially all the remaining economic benefits associated with the asset.



How does the theory apply in this scenario?

Bilbao has retained the right to use the equipment for five of the six years of its remaining useful life, and the asset is expected to have no remaining value at the end of the useful life, which suggests that Bilbao has access to substantially all remaining economic benefits associated with the asset.



Bilbao is also responsible for the insurance and maintenance of the equipment and must continue to lease it even if it becomes obsolete before the expected time. These are risks of ownership.



It therefore appears that Bilbao retains control of the equipment and the transfer is not a sale.



What is the required accounting treatment?

The transaction is a financing agreement in substance and Bilbao Ltd should recognise the proceeds of £750,000 as a loan on 1 July 20X6. It should not de-recognise the underlying asset.



The asset continues to be depreciated over the remaining six years of its useful life, resulting in a depreciation charge of £120,000 per year (£720,000/6 years).



IFRS 9 is applied to the loan liability and it is measured at amortised cost.

What is the deferred tax effect of rolling over the gain?



Solution

The carrying amount of the new land is its cost of £16 million



The tax base of the new land is its cost of £16million less the rolled-over gain of £2.2 million, ie £13.8 million



This gives rise to a temporary difference of £2.2 million.
