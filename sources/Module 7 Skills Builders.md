Scenario 1



You are the financial controller of M Ltd (M) and today you must prepare the statement of cash flows, in accordance with IFRS® Accounting Standards. M uses the indirect method for preparing cash generated from operations.  



To finance its future business ventures, M issued 200,000 £1 ordinary shares during the year for a cash consideration of £2 per share. Expenses were £50,000 and M opted to write these off to share premium.



Further, M obtained new loans of £600,000 during the year. All loans are classified as amortised cost. Effective and nominal interest on all loans is equal and was £120,000 for the year. Finally, the firm issued 250,000 £1 10% debentures at par on 1 January 20X3. Debentures all have a £1 par value and are classified as amortised cost, with effective and nominal interest of 10%. All interest due in the year was paid.



In terms of asset development, M entered into a lease agreement on 31 December 20X3 and obtained a right-of-use asset of £55,000 that was recognised on that date. The first payment is due in more than 12 months from the year end. The corresponding lease payable was accounted for correctly. Right-of-use assets are presented within property, plant and equipment (PPE).



There was also a revaluation of properties during the year. A net revaluation increase of £120,350 was recognised. This was net of deferred tax, at 17%, of £24,650. M does not make annual transfers from the revaluation surplus to retained earnings. It should also be noted that the depreciation charge for the year was £300,000, while the short-term investments are insignificant investments in equity and are not cash equivalents. There was no change in the fair value of any of the short-term investments during the year. No dividends were received on these investments during the year.



Since it was a profitable year for the firm, M declared and paid dividends equal to £48,000 during the year. Finally, due to tax losses carried forward and utilised, no tax was paid in 20X3.
