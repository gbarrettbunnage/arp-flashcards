Question 1 to 3





Rafa Ltd (Rafa) is an oil and gas company that operates in the North Sea.  Rafa applies IFRS Accounting Standards and IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. The oil and gas industry is facing increasing regulatory pressures as a result of government plans to transition to net zero. The directors of Rafa have identified that the changing regulatory environment is an indicator of impairment and have estimated that the recoverable amount of its physical assets are, on average, 40% less than their carrying amounts at 31 March 20X8.



The directors of Rafa do not wish to recognise an impairment nor include information regarding the impact of the regulatory pressures in its sustainability-related financial information. The directors argue that regulatory issues are not within Rafa’s control and, therefore, they do not believe that providing such information provides useful information as to their stewardship of the company.



How should Rafa reflect the impact of the increasing regulatory pressures in its financial statements in accordance with IAS 36 Impairment of Assets?





a) Rafa does not need to recognise an impairment loss, as the directors are correct that they do not need to report information that is not within their control.





b) Rafa should recognise an impairment loss in its statement of profit or loss, providing details in the notes of the financial statements.



c) Rafa should increase the depreciation charge in subsequent periods to reduce their carrying amounts over time.



d) Rafa should recognise an impairment loss by creating a reserve fund that can be used to invest in renewable energy projects, thereby indirectly offsetting the loss through future green initiatives. 



The correct answer is b.



According to IAS 36 Impairment of Assets, when there is an indication that an asset's carrying amount may not be recoverable, the company is required to perform an impairment test. In this scenario, the regulatory risk resulted in the recoverable amount being 40% less than the carrying amount. Therefore, Rafa should recognise an impairment loss in its statement of profit or loss to reflect the recoverable amount of the assets. Providing details of this impairment in the notes to the financial statements ensures transparency and clarity for stakeholders about the impact of regulatory risks on the company's financial position.





Which type of climate-related risk arises as a result of the regulatory pressures?





a) Acute risk



b) Regulatory risk 



c) Chronic risk



d) Transition risk





The correct answer is d.  



Transition risks are those that arise from efforts to transition to a lower-carbon economy. Transition risks include policy and legal risks, such as those arising from the regulatory pressures faced. These risks have financial implications for an entity, such as the costs of asset impairment due to new or amended regulations.



Which TWO of the following would help to ensure consistency between the information disclosed in the note to the financial statements and in the climate-related financial disclosures in respect of the impact of the regulatory pressures?





a) The description of the regulatory pressures provided in the impairment note to the financial statements should use the same terminology as those in the climate-related financial disclosures.



b) The matter should be disclosed only in either the notes to the financial statements or in the climate-related financial disclosures to avoid reporting the information in different places.



c) There should be cross-reference from the impairment note to the financial statements to the climate-related financial disclosures and vice versa to show that the information is connected.



d) The note to the financial statements should include historical information only and the climate-related financial disclosures should include forward-looking information only.



The correct answers are a and c.  



Using consistent terminology and cross-referencing between disclosures helps the users to understand how the financial and non-financial information is related.



Question 4

Mobilink Ltd (Mobilink) is an innovator in smart city solutions and has embarked on a pioneering project to integrate its cutting-edge traffic management systems with renewable energy sources across urban centres. This ambitious initiative aims to reduce urban congestion and pollution by 25% over the next decade. Mobilink is also committed to engaging with the community through educational outreach on sustainable urban living. In line with its commitment to transparency, Mobilink plans to report comprehensively on the project's outcomes in accordance with the Integrated Reporting Framework (IRF).  



Which of the following is essential for ensuring the company's reporting aligns with the IRF's principles on integrated thinking and value creation?





a) Concentrating on the reduction in urban congestion and pollution to highlight the project's environmental benefits, underscoring its contribution to Mobilink's environmental responsibility and sustainability goals.





b) Providing a holistic evaluation of the project’s impacts across various capitals, including its social and relationship capital.







c) Detailing the technological aspects and operational efficiencies of the smart traffic management systems to demonstrate Mobilink's innovation in smart city technologies and its position as an industry leader.





d) Disclosing information only on the financial strategy behind the investment in research and development of its new solutions.



The correct answer is b.



Answer b correctly captures the essence of integrated reporting by advocating for a comprehensive assessment of the project's effects on multiple forms of capital. This multifaceted approach aligns with the principles of the IRF by illustrating how Mobilink creates value through integrated thinking, considering both the immediate and long-term implications of its smart city solutions project on various capitals.



Answers a, c, and d, while they may include important aspects of the project, do not fully embody the integrated thinking approach, as they each focus on a single aspect of the project's impact or outcomes. Integrated reporting requires a broader perspective, considering how different initiatives affect and contribute to the overall value creation process across all capitals.



Questions 5 and 6





Asher Plc (Asher) is a metals and mining corporation which is listed in the UK. It prepares its financial statements in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards and provides a sustainability report prepared in accordance with Global Reporting Initiative (GRI) Standards.



Asher operates in two countries, namely Country A and Country B. Country A has stringent environmental regulations requiring companies to fully remediate any environmental damage they cause. In contrast, Country B's regulations are less strict, allowing for fewer remediation efforts.



Recent operations have led to significant environmental damage in both countries. In Country A, Asher faces a legal obligation to repair damage caused by a contaminated water source, one that is estimated to cost £10 million. In Country B, similar environmental damage has occurred, which is estimated to cost £4 million to repair. Although the law in Country B does not require full remediation, the company has disclosed the risks of potential regulatory changes and damage to its corporate reputation in the future. It has an established history of remediating damage caused by its operations.



What are the requirements for accounting for and disclosing this information under IFRS Accounting Standards ?





a) Environmental costs should not be recognised until the repair costs are incurred.





b) A provision for the £10 million repair costs in Country A, as required by law, but no provision should be made for the £4m costs in Country B, as there is no obligation to repair the damage.





c) Recognise an environmental liability provision of £14 million, covering both the legal obligation in Country A and the constructive obligation.





d) Recognise an environmental liability provision of £4 million, covering only the constructive obligation in Country B.



The correct answer is c.



Asher has a legal obligation in Country A and a constructive obligation in Country B. A provision should be recognised in respect of both legal and constructive obligations.



Which are the requirements for disclosing this information in the Strategic Report?





a) There is no requirement to provide information relating to the environmental damage in the Strategic Report as a separate sustainability report is prepared.





b) The Strategic Report should include information relating to the environmental damage as it represents a climate-related risk and should be prepared in accordance with TCFD recommendations.



The correct answer is b.



The future repair costs represent a climate-related risk that will impact on the prospects of the entity. They should be discussed in the Strategic Report. Preparation of a separate sustainability report is not relevant when considering what should be included in the Strategic Report.



Question 7

Agrocorp Ltd (Agrocorp) is an agricultural company which is facing increasing physical risks from climate change, including water scarcity. While water scarcity is not impacting the company at present, the risks threaten the company's future crop production which could significantly impact its ability to fulfil its contracts with customers.



In compliance with the UK regulatory requirements, Agrocorp adheres to the Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) recommendations and has included its climate-related financial disclosures in the Non-Financial and Sustainability Information Statement section of the Strategic Report for the year 20X3. Among these disclosures, Agrocorp provides detailed analysis on how climate-related risks affect the company’s business strategy and financial planning.



How should Agrocorp ensure consistency between its TCFD-aligned climate-related financial disclosures and its financial reporting in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards? Select ALL that apply.





a) Adjust its revenue recognition accounting policies to reflect future physical risks.





b) Provide detailed notes accompanying the financial statements that discuss the accounting policies, assumptions, methodologies, and potential financial impacts.





c) Recognise impairment losses on assets that are most likely to be affected by the identified climate-related risks in the future.  





d) Disclose the financial impact of potential water scarcity in the climate-related financial disclosures.





e) Disclose the potential impact on local communities of the use of water in a situation of water scarcity in the climate-related financial disclosures.



The correct answers are b and d.



Answer b effectively communicates the complexity and uncertainty of forecasting the financial impacts of climate change, providing users with the detailed context necessary to understand the financial position of Agrocorp.



Answer d is required in disclosures compliant with TCFD as this information provides users with an understanding of climate-related risks. Option a is incorrect as revenue recognition should reflect the requirements of IFRS 15 and should not anticipate future changes in costs or market conditions.



Answer c is incorrect, as there is no evidence that the recoverable amount of Agrocorp’s assets has fallen below its carrying amount.



Answer e is incorrect as there is no requirement to disclose the impact on stakeholders such as local communities in financial disclosures.



Question 8

Daria Ltd (Daria) is a manufacturing company that specialises in outdoor sports equipment. In its sustainability-related disclosures, which are prepared in accordance with IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards, the company disclosed its quantitative and qualitative assessments of climate-related financial risks.  



Among those risks, Daria indicates physical risks (including erratic weather patterns and natural disasters) as long-term risks which will impact the company in more than 10 years with a low possibility of materialising, with the company noting that the net realisable value of inventory will fall by £0.8 million if the risk materialises.



Further, Daria reports on the transitional risk of evolving consumer demand towards environmentally sustainable products as short-term risk which will impact the company within the next 12 months, with a high possibility of materialising. The report highlights that this shift in consumer preferences resulted in a £1.2 million reduction in the net realisable value of its inventory.



Considering the climate-related risks and their quantified potential impacts on inventory, how should Daria Ltd reflect these issues in its financial statements for this year?





a) Postpone any recognition of inventory write-down until there is objective evidence that either risk will materialise in the near future





b) Recognise write-down equal to £0.8 million due to physical risks





c) Recognise write-down equal to £1.2 million due to transitional risks





d) Recognise write-down equal to £2 million due to physical and transitional risks



The correct answer is c.



This option is the most appropriate, given Daria ’s specific assessment of the risks. Recognising a write-down of £1.2 million due to transitional risks is in line with the company’s evaluation of these risks as being highly likely to materialise and affect the company in the next 12 months. This action ensures that the financial statements accurately reflect the most immediate and quantifiable impacts on the company's inventory, adhering to the principles of relevance and faithful representation in financial reporting.
