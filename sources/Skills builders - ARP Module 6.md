Aikido Ltd (Aikido) is considering the acquisition of Fitness Ltd (Fitness) and acquires 25% of Fitness’ voting shares, with an option to purchase an additional 4% in the next 2 years. During the acquisition negotiations, Aikido secured an agreement that will provide it with control over the financial and operating policies of Fitness. Aikido also realised that Shogun Ltd (Shogun) holds a 20% stake in Fitness, with significant influence. The two parties agreed to a memorandum of understanding, stipulating that Fitness will vote on behalf of Shogun. 



Based on IFRS 10, does Aikido have control over Fitness? 



Unselected

a) 



Yes, because it has significant influence and potential to acquire additional voting shares 



Unselected

b) 



No, because it does not control more than 50% of the voting rights



Correct AnswerUnselected

c) 



&#x20;Yes, because it has control over the financial and operating policies, affecting Fitness' returns



Unselected

d) 



No, because all shareholders must agree on key operational decisions 





Hide question 1 feedback

Feedback

The correct answer is c. 



Aikido has control over Fitness according to IFRS 10. Despite owning only 25% of the voting shares, with an option for an additional 4%, the key factor is the agreement granting Aikido control over Fitness' financial and operating policies. Furthermore, the memorandum of understanding with Shogun, allowing Aikido to vote on Shogun's behalf, strengthens Aikido's position to direct Fitness' relevant activities and affect its returns, satisfying IFRS 10's control criteria, but it is not necessary for control. As a standalone event, though, it wouldn't suffice for Aikido to gain control of Fitness.

What is the relationship Keystone has with Letterman, Shipmaker and Caledon at 31 December 20X3?

Feedback

The correct answer is d.



Letterman 



Keystone owns 59% (1,000,000/1,700,000) of the total number of shares but only 45% (1,000,000/2,200,000) of the votes. The power to control is usually based on voting rights.  



Keystone, therefore, does not control Letterman and it is not a subsidiary.  



It may qualify as an associate if significant influence exists.



Shipmaker 



Keystone owns 40% (4,000,000/10,000,000) of the shares in issue and is not, therefore, a subsidiary on this basis.  



Keystone, however, has substantive potential voting rights through the currently convertible bonds. 



These give voting power of 57% (\[4,000,000 + 4,000,000]/\[10,000,000 + 4,000,000]) and, therefore, the power to direct the relevant activities of Shipmaker. 



Keystone controls Shipmaker and it is a subsidiary. For consolidation purposes, the group share is 40% and the NCI is 60% (ie based on the actual voting rights).



Caledon 



Keystone owns 41% (2,500,000/6,000,000) of the shares of Caledon.  



When (or if) the options are exercised, Keystone will own 53% (\[2,500,000 + 1,500,000]/\[6,000,000 + 1,500,000]) and Caledon would be a subsidiary.



However, the options are not currently exercisable and therefore these are not substantive potential voting rights. 



Caledon is therefore not a subsidiary at the reporting date but may be an associate if significant influence is evident.
