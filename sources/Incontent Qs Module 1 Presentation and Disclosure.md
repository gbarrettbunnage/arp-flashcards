### Lesson 1: Accounting Principles and the Conceptual Framework



Investments Ltd is preparing its financial statements and needs to determine the fair value of several assets and liabilities. The company holds an unquoted investment in a start-up, a property in a prime location, and shares in a FTSE 100 listed company. As of the measurement date, the FTSE 100 shares have a market price of £50,000, the start-up investment's latest income valuation was estimated at £200,000, and a similar property in the same location was recently sold for £300,000.



According to IFRS 13, how should Investments Ltd classify the fair value inputs for these assets?





a) The FTSE 100 shares at Level 1, the start-up investment at Level 2, and the property at Level 3.





b) The FTSE 100 shares at Level 1, the start-up investment at Level 3, and the property at Level 2.





c)  All assets at Level 1 as they have specific market-based valuations.





d)  The FTSE 100 shares at Level 2, the start-up investment at Level 1, and the property at Level 3.



The correct answer is b.







According to IFRS 13, fair value inputs are classified into three levels in the fair value hierarchy. Level 1 inputs are unadjusted quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities (such as the FTSE 100 shares). Level 2 inputs are inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly or indirectly (such as the property, where a similar property's sale value can be observed). Level 3 inputs are unobservable inputs for the asset or liability (such as the start-up investment, where the valuation is based on income estimates and not market data). Therefore, option b correctly classifies the fair value inputs for these assets according to IFRS 13.







Option a is incorrect as it misclassifies the start-up investment and the property. Option c is incorrect as it doesn't recognise the different levels of inputs for these assets. Option d is incorrect as it misclassifies all three assets.

### Lesson 2: Presentation of Financial Statements, Including Interim Reporting



Green Ltd (Green), an environmental consulting firm, is preparing its statement of financial position in accordance with IFRS 18. The company has several different types of assets and liabilities. Which of the following approaches should Green adopt for presenting its statement of financial position to ensure users' understanding of the entity's financial position?





a) Present all assets and liabilities in a single line item each to maintain simplicity in the statement.





b) Group assets and liabilities into broad categories without further breakdowns or sub-classifications.





c) Provide additional line items, headings, and subtotals, and break down line items as necessary to enhance users’ understanding of the financial position.





d) Only disclose detailed breakdowns of assets and liabilities in a separate report, not in the statement of financial position or its notes.



The correct answer is c.



&#x20;



According to IFRS 18, the presentation of a statement of financial position should provide information that is relevant and facilitates understanding of an entity's financial position. This often requires entities to present additional line items, headings, and subtotals on the face of the statement of financial position (disaggregation). Moreover, for line items containing a variety of different balances, further sub-classifications should be provided as appropriate, either on the face of the statement or in the notes. This is to aid users in understanding the entity's financial position, aligning with option (c).



&#x20;



Options (a) and (b) are incorrect because they oversimplify the presentation and may not provide sufficient detail to understand the entity's financial position. Option (d) is incorrect as detailed breakdowns should be part of the statement of financial position or its notes, not in a separate report.







Textile Manufacturing Ltd has several financial transactions in the year 20X8, including a revaluation gain of its buildings and regular sales and administrative expenses. In preparing its financial statements in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards, how should these items be presented in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income?





a) Include both the revaluation gain and regular sales and administrative expenses in the profit or loss section.





b) Report the revaluation gain in other comprehensive income and the regular sales and administrative expenses in the profit or loss section.





c) Present the revaluation gain in the profit or loss section and the regular sales and administrative expenses in other comprehensive income.





d) Exclude both the revaluation gain and regular sales and administrative expenses from the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, reporting them separately.



The correct answer is b.







According to IFRS Accounting Standards, the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income comprises two main components: profit or loss, and other comprehensive income (OCI). Profit or loss includes regular transactions, such as sales, cost of sales and administrative expenses. Other comprehensive income includes items not recognised in profit or loss, such as revaluation gains as per IAS 16. Therefore, the revaluation gain should be reported in OCI and accumulated in equity as a revaluation surplus, while regular sales and administrative expenses should be included in the profit or loss section, aligning with option b.







Option a is incorrect because revaluation gains are reported in OCI, not in profit or loss. Option c is incorrect as it suggests the opposite of the required treatment. Option d is incorrect because both types of items should be included in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, but in different sections.

Tech Ltd (Tech) is preparing its interim financial report for the first half of the financial year 20X9. During this period, the company reported revenue of £500,000 and expenses totalling £300,000. Additionally, it anticipates annual taxation of £100,000 and a volume-based supplier discount that is expected to total £20,000 for the year. According to IAS 34 Interim Financial Reporting, which of the following should Tech include in its interim financial report?





a) Revenue of £500,000 and expenses of £300,000, with taxation reported at £50,000 (half of the annual anticipated taxation) and the full supplier discount of £20,000.





b) Revenue of £1m (estimated annual revenue) and expenses of £600,000 (estimated annual expenses), with no inclusion of taxation or supplier discounts.





c) Revenue of £500,000 and expenses of £300,000, with no inclusion of taxation or supplier discounts as these are calculated on an annual basis.





d) Revenue of £500,000 and expenses of £300,000, with taxation reported at £50,000 (half of the annual anticipated taxation) and a supplier discount of £10,000 (half of the annual anticipated discount).



The correct answer is d.







According to IAS 34, the interim financial report should treat the interim period as a separate accounting period. Thus, actual figures for revenue and expenses for the 6-month period should be reported. Items that are typically calculated on an annual basis, such as taxation or volume-based supplier discounts, should be included proportionally. In the case of Tech, this means reporting revenue of £500,000 and expenses of £300,000 as actual figures for the interim period. The annual anticipated taxation of £100,000 should be included proportionally, ie £50,000 for the 6-month period. Similarly, the volume-based supplier discount expected to total £20,000 for the year should be reported as £10,000 (half for the interim period). This aligns with option d.







Option a is incorrect as it includes the full annual supplier discount. Option b is incorrect as it estimates annual figures instead of using actual interim figures. Option c is incorrect because it omits the proportional inclusion of items calculated on an annual basis.



#### Lesson 3: IAS 8 Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements



R\&D Ltd, a company engaged in the development of innovative biotechnology, is preparing its financial statements. The company has incurred substantial costs for a project that does not fit precisely under any existing IFRS Accounting Standard. In choosing an appropriate accounting policy for these costs, what should the directors of R\&D Ltd primarily consider according to IAS 8?





a) The company's past accounting practices and policies for similar expenditures.





b) The accounting policies of other biotechnology companies for such costs.





c) An IFRS Accounting Standard dealing with similar and related issues and the definitions, recognition criteria, and measurement concepts in the Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting.





d) The preference of the company's auditors and the expectations of the majority of the company's shareholders.



The correct answer is c.







According to IAS 8, when no specific standard or interpretation directly applies to a transaction or event, the directors should choose an accounting policy that ensures the information is relevant and reliable. In making this judgement, they should first consider standards dealing with similar and related issues, and then refer to the definitions, recognition criteria and measurement concepts in the Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting. This helps ensure that the chosen policy aligns with the principles and guidelines that underpin IFRS Accounting Standards, providing consistency and comparability in financial reporting. This aligns with option c.







Option a is not the primary consideration, as past practices may not always align with IFRS principles. Option b might provide some insights but is not the recommended approach according to IAS 8. Option d is incorrect as the preferences of auditors or shareholder expectations are not the basis for selecting accounting policies under IAS 8.

Reliable Products Ltd, a technology firm, has recently revised its estimates regarding the warranty provisions for its products. Historically, the company estimated that 2% of its sales would require warranty services. However, due to improved quality control measures and historical data analysis, the company has now revised this estimate to 1% of its sales. According to IAS 8, how should Reliable Products Ltd account for this change in estimate?





a) Treat it as a change in accounting policy and restate prior period financial statements accordingly.





b) Recognise it as a correction of an error and adjust the opening balance of retained earnings for the earliest period presented.





c) Apply the change prospectively, adjusting the warranty provision only for sales made after the change in estimate.





d) Disclose it as a contingent liability in the notes to the financial statements and not adjust the warranty provision.





The correct answer is c.







According to IAS 8, a change in accounting estimates, such as a revision of warranty provision rates, should be accounted for prospectively. This means that Reliable Products Ltd should apply the new estimate of 1% to sales occurring after the change. There is no need to restate previous financial statements, as this change is not a correction of an error or a change in accounting policy. Thus, option c is the correct approach.







Option a is incorrect as this is not a change in accounting policy. Option b is incorrect because this scenario does not represent the correction of an error. Option d is also incorrect as this is not a contingent liability, but an actual change in accounting estimate that should be reflected in the financial statements.







During the preparation of the 20X5 financial statements, it was discovered that Peter Ltd (Peter) incorrectly recorded directors' remuneration in the 20X3 financial statements. Due to a computational error, the remuneration was understated by £20,000. This error went unnoticed and was not corrected in the 20X4 financial statements. According to IAS 8, how should Peter correct this material error?



a) Adjust the directors' remuneration figure in the 20X5 financial statements and reflect the impact in the current year's profit or loss.





b) Restate the comparative amounts for the prior period (20X3) in the 20X5 financial statements, correcting the directors' remuneration and affected profit figures.





c) Treat it as a change in accounting estimate and apply the correction prospectively from the year 20X5.





d) Disclose the error in the notes to the 20X5 financial statements but make no adjustments to the figures for the previous years.





The correct answer is b.







IAS 8 requires that errors in the financial statements of prior periods be corrected by restating the comparative amounts for the period(s) in which the error occurred. In this case, the error discovered affects the financial statements for the year 20X3. Therefore, the company should restate its 20X3 financial statements in its 20X5 report to correct the understatement of directors' remuneration and the consequent overstatement of profit.







Option a is incorrect because merely adjusting the error in the 20X5 financial statements does not correct the financial statements for the year in which the error occurred.







Option c is incorrect as this scenario is not a change in accounting estimate but an error correction.

Viktor Ltd (Viktor) operates numerous facilities for its business operations. The directors are considering adopting a policy of revaluing its facilities as of 31 July 20X5. The facilities are currently measured at historical cost. They are seeking your advice on the impact of this policy change on the financial statements for the year ending 31 July 20X5, including any effect on the 20X4 comparatives.



What impact does the change in accounting policy to revalue the facilities have on the comparatives for the year 20X4 in Viktor 's financial statements?





a) The comparatives for 20X4 should be restated to reflect the new accounting policy as if it had always been applied.





b) The comparatives for 20X4 should not be adjusted, nor is any disclosure required; only the future financial statements will reflect the new policy.





c) Only the depreciation expense for 20X4 should be adjusted in the comparatives, leaving other figures unchanged.





d) The comparatives for 20X4 should not be adjusted, however, the 20X5 financial statements must be prepared on both the historical cost basis and the revaluation basis.





The correct answer is b.

Dilution Ltd is preparing its year-end financial statements. During the year, Dilution Ltd reported a profit of £500,000. The company had 200,000 weighted average shares outstanding throughout the year. Additionally, it had issued options that could potentially dilute EPS in the future, but these were out of the money at the year end. How should Dilution Ltd disclose its EPS information according to IAS 33?





a) Present both basic and diluted EPS, including a reconciliation to profit for the period, and mention the anti-dilutive instruments in the notes.





b) Only present basic EPS as diluted EPS is not relevant due to the out-of-the-money options.





c) Present diluted EPS only, considering the potential impact of the out-of-the-money options.





d) Dilution Ltd should not present EPS as FRS 102 does not provide guidance on the calculation and disclosure of earnings per share.





The correct answer is a.



&#x20;



IAS 33 requires the presentation of basic and diluted EPS. Even if the diluted EPS is not materially different from the basic EPS (eg due to the presence of anti-dilutive instruments such as out-of-the-money options), the company is still required to present both. The company should also provide a reconciliation of the profit or loss for the period to the basic and diluted EPS. Additionally, information about instruments that could potentially dilute EPS in the future, such as out-of-the-money options, should be disclosed in the notes. This aligns with option a.



&#x20;



Option b is incorrect because both basic and diluted EPS should be presented, regardless of whether the diluted EPS is materially different at the year end. Option c is incorrect as it suggests presenting diluted EPS only. Option d is incorrect because even though FRS 102 does not provide guidance on EPS, IAS 33 does, and Dilution Ltd should refer to IAS 33 for such disclosures.
